Last session: retrieve the right passages, then let the model answer from them. We built exactly that, basic RAG, and pointed it at three real reports. Here is what it did.

Long report · World Bank · 200 pages

A confident wrong number

Question

What is the growth outlook for South Asia in this report?

Basic RAG answered Wrong
"The report projects that South Asia's real GDP growth will rise to about 4 percent in 2026 (up from 3.9 percent in 2025) …"
The report says, page 96
"Growth in SAR is expected to soften to 6.3 percent in 2026 …"

All five passages came from the right pages. None held the outlook sentence: they were fragments of tables.

Visual · Apple iMac report · 12 pages

The answer is in a chart

Question

What was the baseline emissions figure, and how many kilograms did the reductions remove?

Basic RAG answered Missed
"… a baseline emissions figure of 445 kg CO₂e … but it does not give the exact kilogram amount removed."
The report says, chart on page 2
445 kg baseline, minus 99 kg of reductions, gives 346 kg.

Text extraction keeps the chart's labels, not what they add up to. A footnote question on page 1 got "I could not find this in the document."

Each document breaks basic RAG in a different way. Today we fix each one with a small, specific technique, and measure what it changes. The same questions again, at the end.

          What's next
          Beyond today
          
  
          
    
          More query transformations
          Multi-query (ask several phrasings), HyDE (search with a drafted answer), decomposition (split a two-part question). Same place in the pipeline as our LLM step.
          
    
          Agentic and corrective RAG
          Let the model grade what it retrieved and search again when it is not enough. Turns the straight pipeline into a loop.
          
    
          GraphRAG
          Extract entities and relations, then answer questions that span many documents ("which suppliers appear in both reports?").
          
    
          Evaluation at scale
          We used 37 hand-checked questions. In production, add an LLM-as-judge on answers and track retrieval metrics on every change.
          
    
          Long context instead?
          One 12-page PDF fits in a prompt. Thousands of documents do not, cost grows per question, and models still miss buried facts. Retrieval also gives citations.
          
    
          Frameworks
          LangChain, rebuilt with the same parts, scored MRR 0.95 against our 0.74 on the GPO report, thanks to its smaller default chunks. No framework did the parsing in chapters 2 and 3. Measure.
          
  
          

          


          

          Every number on this page was measured on 37 hand-checked questions across the three reports. Models: BAAI bge-small embeddings, Qdrant BM25, MiniLM cross-encoder (all local), Groq gpt-oss-120b for answers, Gemini Flash-Lite for vision.
